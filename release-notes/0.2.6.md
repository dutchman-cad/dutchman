Dutchman Convert 0.2.6 converts SolidWorks assemblies into SolidCraft designs, accepts whole projects as folders or ZIPs, and helps you find conversion problems locally without an AI assistant. More assembly placements, parameter edits, and feature timelines carry across, with SolidCraft 0.30 as the newest known target.

## What's new

- **SolidWorks assemblies.** Convert `.sldasm` files with their external parts and nested sub-assemblies. Dutchman searches the project and extra search folders, matches file names without regard to case, and reports missing parts while converting the rest. Repeated references to the saved active configuration share one read.
- **Whole projects from a folder or ZIP.** See the project inventory before converting, keep referenced parts with their assemblies, and convert unused parts separately. A same-name STEP export beside a native file is a fallback when native conversion fails. ZIP extraction has entry and size limits.
- **Find a failure without an AI assistant.** `dutchman diagnose --narrow` isolates a failing part and feature, resumes interrupted work, and gives you a recipe for a small rebuild. Check whether that rebuild fails the same way, then make a local report ZIP to review and share. The desktop app offers the same steps from a failed file's card.
- **Check a folder with doctor.** `dutchman doctor` checks a folder or ZIP within CPU and memory budgets, resumes completed checks, and groups repeated findings. Compare a design with its matching STEP export to investigate shape and placement differences. These comparisons are advisory.
- **Choose what to report.** Review an anonymous summary before sending it, or follow the private-file sharing steps. Diagnosis and doctor keep their results local; making a report ZIP does not send it. A rebuild ZIP contains geometry, so review it before sharing. Report sending asks for consent and confirms delivery only after a matching receipt.
- **Better placements, parameter edits, and shorter timelines.** Mirrored components keep their reflected shape, and joined components carry the parts placed inside them. More feature distances, angles, and sketch dimensions keep their parameter expressions, and nested profile loops keep their holes. Repeated assembly parts share a definition, sketches sit under their parts, and redundant imported geometry is left out when base features already hold all the bodies.
- **SolidCraft 0.30.** Without an installed SolidCraft CLI, Dutchman now defaults to the 0.30 series, and on 0.30 hidden history copies stay out of the History bar. The supported window is 0.18–0.30, with 0.18.2 as the verification floor; designs keep format 23.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| RailCore II 300ZL | 379 of 379 | 3:10 | 3.9 GB |
| RailCore II 300ZLT | 396 of 396 | 3:53 | 3.9 GB |
| Voron Trident | 1,697 of 1,697 | 11:38 | 16.9 GB |

A public sweep of 656 files converts 652. Reports are limited to 25 MB per bundle, about 5 an hour, and 200 a day.

## Known issues

- SolidWorks mates are not converted, and most of a large SolidWorks assembly converts as geometry: on one public 88-component assembly, all 88 parts placed and about 13% of its features stayed editable. Some of its parts come through as rough surfaces, and a few of its part files fail when converted on their own.
- A Fusion design with Remove features can keep the removed bodies.
- The Voron Trident converts as geometry without editable history. Assemblies as large as the Voron V1.8 and 2.4 need more than 16 GB of memory.
- The estimate before a conversion can still be well under the real peak on large assemblies; a run that won't fit stops early or skips the history check and says so.
- STEP comparison and `doctor` findings are advisory: their false-positive rate on large assemblies is not established.
- The RailCore timeline stays long on SolidCraft older than 0.30.
