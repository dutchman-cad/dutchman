Dutchman Convert turns designs from other CAD programs into native SolidCraft designs, keeping as much of their feature history, sketches, and assemblies as it can. 0.2.5 converts a public 350-occurrence Fusion printer frame with every part placed and editable history where it maps, works on the parts of an assembly in parallel, lets you choose what to keep with a memory and time estimate first, and can write an anonymous diagnostic log while it runs.

## What's new

- **Fusion assemblies keep each part's history and placement.** Each component keeps the features, sketches, and parameters that build its own bodies, and Dutchman maps and checks them part by part. The result on a public 350-occurrence Fusion printer frame: 379 of 379 placements placed, 153 of 416 features come across, and 19 parts fully editable. The JSON report lists each part under `history.parts`, with its first blocker. On the public Voron 2.4: 1,683 of 1,683 placements placed, with every body in its own colour; it was imported as one grey solid before. Component bodies and faces keep their colour and transparency bindings, including stored and imported solids; bindings left out are reported. A joint that conflicts with the stored placements is kept suppressed, so opening the design leaves its parts where they belong. Parts inside inserted Fusion designs keep the placements their poses are worked out from.


- **Large assemblies convert much faster.** Dutchman now prepares an assembly's parts on several cores, with output identical to a single-threaded run, and keeps enough memory back that a computer with less to spare simply uses fewer cores (`--threads 1` runs one at a time). It also no longer copies the whole design for each part, and reads a design inside an archive that fails to read once instead of at every place it is used. The printer frame converts with its history checks in under 5 minutes, down from over 14 in 0.2.4, and a 33-placement assembly in 5 minutes instead of 9½ at about the same peak memory (one thread: 7 minutes, with 3 GB less).
- **Choose what to keep, and see the estimate first.** Pick Fast (the shape only), Standard, or Full, or switch feature history, sketches, and joints off under Advanced, in the desktop app, with `--preset` and per-choice flags, or in MCP. Before converting, Dutchman estimates peak memory and time from a quick read of the file, using estimates refitted against measured conversions. Memory and time show separate uncertainty ranges where measured; time estimates account for startup and assembly workers. When that would not fit in the memory your computer has free, it offers a lighter preset before decoding. Standard leaves feature history out only when it would not fit and the lighter conversion would, and says so. Small estimates read as under 0.1 GB and under a minute.
- **A diagnostic log you can share.** Tick "Save a diagnostic log" in the app, or pass `--diagnostics`, and Dutchman writes an anonymous log as it runs, so even a run you stop or that crashes leaves everything up to that moment. It records versions, your system's cores and memory, each stage, each part of the archive by a scrambled id with its timing, and memory and CPU every 30 seconds; it never holds file or folder names, part or feature names, messages, or geometry. Resource readings work on Windows and macOS too. Logs are kept in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Dutchman\diagnostics` on Windows, `~/Library/Logs/Dutchman` on macOS, and `~/.local/state/dutchman/diagnostics` on Linux, newest ten. If a run didn't finish, the app points you to its log the next time it starts.
- **Updates from the desktop app.** The app checks for stable releases at startup and hourly. Its update button offers release notes and the right download or package manager command, with choices to skip a version or ask again in three days. Homebrew updates show live progress and restart controls; Scoop can close, update, and reopen the app. The header puts Report a problem beside the theme switch, memory warnings are shorter, and a SolidCraft version pill names the installed release and opens compatibility guidance and target settings.
- **Finds and targets your SolidCraft.** Dutchman targets the SolidCraft it finds on your computer, or else the newest release series it knows (0.27). On Windows it now also finds SolidCraft wherever its installer put it. Output uses `.sc` for SolidCraft 0.25.1 and newer, and `.solidcraft` for older targets.
- **More files and editable features.** Fusion bodies that a Combine used up as a tool, or that a Remove step took out of the design, no longer come back as extra bodies. Fusion designs saved while Fusion was offline now open. FreeCAD TechDraw pages convert to DXF with `--to dxf`. Inventor splits by a work plane map to SolidCraft splits, derived parts keep their stored bodies, and schema-15 parts are read. One-sided partial solid revolutions and uniform periodic sketch B-splines keep editable history where they map, and circular profile holes survive unused layout lines crossing them. SolidWorks rolling-ball blends reach the exported geometry. Progress shows long runs in hours and minutes.

## Known issue

A very large Fusion assembly can need more than 24 GB of memory to convert ([#227](https://github.com/dutchman-cad/dutchman/issues/227)). The estimate warns before a run that may not fit; a run can still need more memory than estimated.
