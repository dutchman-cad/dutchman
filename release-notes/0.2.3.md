Dutchman Convert turns designs from other CAD programs into native SolidCraft designs, keeping as much of their feature history, sketches, and assemblies as it can. 0.2.3 reads again the SolidWorks parts that 0.2.0 to 0.2.2 read empty or incomplete, places Fusion assemblies much closer to how they were designed, and keeps more FreeCAD and Inventor history.

## What's new

- **SolidWorks parts read again.** The parts behind the known issue in 0.2.0 to 0.2.2, which came out empty, with fewer bodies, or failing the edge checks, convert as they did in 0.1.5. Solid snapshots that SolidWorks stores beside its features are no longer read as extra sketches.
- **Fusion assemblies placed as designed.** Designs inserted at the top of an assembly keep their current placement instead of the one they had when first inserted. A joint between a component and a part inside it, as every rigid group stores, now moves the part and leaves the component where the design puts it. A copy of a component placed more than once is held once, even when a rigid group holds both the copy and a part inside it. Rigid-group members mate the way Fusion mates them, and joints to parts inside nested and merged designs find those parts.
- **More FreeCAD history and geometry.** Fillets and chamfers on the edges of the step before them map into the history. Parts with trimmed elliptical edges no longer lose the faces those edges bound.
- **Inventor mirrors keep their history.** A mirror of features about a work plane converts as a SolidCraft mirror pattern.
- **Incomplete models keep what they can.** When only some faces of a body can be written, those faces convert as surfaces and the report lists the rest.

## Known issue

A very large Fusion assembly can need more than 24 GB of memory to convert ([#227](https://github.com/dutchman-cad/dutchman/issues/227)); on a machine with less, the system stops the conversion before it writes anything.
